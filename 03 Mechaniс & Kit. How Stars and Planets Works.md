### Dynamic Gas Exchange

Under the conditions of the Living World, the complete cooling and solidification of the interiors of small transuranic planets (such as Lesoto, Mammonia, and Del-Alfomar) leads to the disappearance of the classical dynamo effect and the total loss of a global magnetic field. However, contrary to the laws of the real ΛCDM Universe—where such a scenario dictates the inevitable stripping of the atmosphere—the physics of the Living World establishes a unique phenomenon of **dynamic flowing equilibrium** between the planet's thermosphere and the oncoming river of dense, non-radioactive plasma.

---

**1. The Mass Balance Equation at the Exosphere Boundary**

The atmosphere of a transuranic dwarf world exists in a dynamic rather than static equilibrium with the surrounding space environment. The cumulative mass of the atmospheric envelope M_atm is strictly regulated via a balanced mass flux equation:

dM_atm / dt = M_capture - M_strip

Where M_capture represents the rate of gravitational capture of ions from the oncoming stellar wind, and M_strip represents the rate of mechanical stripping (the effect of viscous shear friction) of gases by the plasma stream.

---

**2. The Process of Gravitational Matter Capture (M_capture)**

As the dense plasma stream, moving at a moderate velocity (100–250 km/s), encounters the planet, it enters its zone of gravitational control—the expansive Hill Sphere provided by the ultra-dense transuranic core (Hofitum-125).

- **Recombination and Deceleration**: Hydrogen and helium ions from the wind collide with the upper, rarefied layers of the planetary thermosphere. This induces a cascade of particle collisions. The dense wind is effectively decelerated against the planet's gaseous cushion. Charged ions capture free electrons (undergo recombination), transforming into neutral atoms.
    
- **Gravitational Settling**: Losing their kinetic energy, these cooled neutral gases can no longer overcome the escape velocity of the dwarf world, which, due to the transuranic core, is equivalent to Earth's (11.2 km/s). The 1g gravitational pull forces them to settle downward, continuously replenishing and updating the composition of the planetary atmosphere.
    

**3. The Process of Mechanical Gas Stripping (M_strip)**

Simultaneously, an opposing vector operates. The oncoming plasma flow, driven by viscous hydrodynamic friction, drags the outer edge of the planetary exosphere along with it, "combing away" and carrying a fraction of the lighter gases back into interplanetary space.

---

**4. Self-Regulation and the Balancing Valve Principle**

The gas exchange system possesses absolute trigger stability, functioning as an automatic pressure-regulating valve:

- **Over-Density Scenario**: If the planet's atmosphere becomes excessively dense, its physical volume expands. The upper boundary of the exosphere rises higher into space, extending beyond the core's primary gravitational defenses. The oncoming stellar wind begins to shear the gas away with increased force. The stripping rate (M_strip) instantly exceeds the capture rate, returning the atmosphere to its nominal parameters.

- **Under-Density Scenario**: If the atmosphere becomes rarefied, its volume contracts. The gaseous blanket shrinks downward, sheltering deep within the gravitational well of the core. The stellar wind can no longer reach or scrape away the gas molecules, yet the Hill Sphere continues to passively pull in passing ions. The capture rate (M_capture) begins to prevail, restoring the atmospheric density.
